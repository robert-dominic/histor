# Content integrity

Histor is about telling the record apart from the legend. Content that blurs them breaks the product.

## Every piece of evidence

- Has exactly one status: `record`, `disputed` or `legend`.
- Has at least one source, with a title, the kind of source and a link or citation.
  - Kinds: `testimony` (sworn statements), `official` (court, consular, insurance records), `secondary` (historians, museums, journalism), `fiction` (novels, stories, films).
- A `legend` item names where the legend came from, such as the 1884 Conan Doyle story.
- A `disputed` item states both sides in one line each.
- Holds the historians' reading: which theories it supports and which it weakens.

## Writing rules

1. **Never invent facts, names, dates, quotes or sources.** If something isn't in a source, it doesn't go in.
2. **Paraphrase by default.** Quote only from primary sources, keep quotes short, and cite them.
3. **Mark uncertainty.** If sources disagree on a date or detail, say so in the content.
4. **Theories:** never present one as proven. Standings are `leading`, `considered` or `rejected`, with a one-line reason.
5. **Respect the dead.** Ten real people were lost on the Mary Celeste, including a two-year-old. The voice can be wry about legends and the people who invented them. It is never flippant about the victims or their families.
6. **Images:** use public-domain historical images (with their source), or clearly label an image as an illustration. Never present an illustration or AI image as a photograph or a historical document.
7. **Sound:** period-appropriate means sounds of things that existed at the time. Never imply that an audio clip is a historical recording.
